Early accessThis guide covers what is available today. Payroll processing is not available yet.

Job roles

A job role describes what someone does at your company, for example Payroll Specialist. Each role has a title, a department, a full description of its duties and qualifications, and a status.

Job roles are not access roles

A job role never changes what a person can do in the product, and choosing one never sets anyone’s pay. What people can do is controlled by their access role. See Users, roles, and permissions.

Open the Roles page

Section titled “Open the Roles page”

In the side menu choose People → Roles → Roles. The page is titled Roles and lists each role with its Internal Code, Department, Job Family, Level, Employees Assigned, Pay Type, Employer Pay Band, Benchmark Status, Status, and Effective Date. The table is wide, so it scrolls sideways; the role’s title and the Actions button stay in view.

  • Search matches a role’s title, internal code, job family, or department.
  • Filters: Department, Job family, Level, Status, and Pay type. Select Clear filters to start over. Use Sort by to reorder the list.
  • Rows per page: choose 10, 25, or 50, and use Previous and Next to move between pages.
  • Pay Type is how the role is usually paid, such as hourly or salary. It is guidance only. A role has no pay rate.
  • Employer Pay Band is your company’s own pay range for the role, shown as a minimum and a maximum with “/ yr” or “/ hr” after it, or Not set. See Compensation below.
  • Benchmark Status says whether market benchmark figures can be shown for the role: Available, No SOC code, or No data imported. It shows a dash when your access doesn’t include market benchmarks.

A role is always one of three statuses, shown as a word in the Status column:

Status Meaning
Draft Still being written. It can be edited, but it can’t be given to an employee and it doesn’t appear when you add an employee.
Active Published. It can be given to employees and appears in Add Employee.
Archived Retired. It keeps its history and any existing assignments, and no longer appears when you add an employee.

Add a role

Section titled “Add a role”

Select Add Role to describe a role yourself, or Use EPN Template to start from one of the roles EPN provides. A guided window walks you through six steps:

  1. Start. Choose Use an EPN template and pick one, or Start from a blank role. A template is copied into your own role. Changing your copy never changes the EPN template.
  2. Role details. Enter the Role title (required), and optionally a short Description, an Internal code that is unique in your company, a Department, Job family, Level, and a Summary.
  3. Responsibilities. Enter the purpose, the essential duties, other responsibilities, expected outcomes, and who the role reports to. Enter one item per line in the list fields.
  4. Qualifications. Enter education, experience, licenses, certifications, skills, and working conditions, plus Employment guidance: the typical employment type and pay type (choose from the list, or keep a value that was entered earlier), and employer classification details.
  5. Compensation (optional). Add an employer pay band for the role and look at market benchmarks. You can skip this step: a role can always be saved and activated without a pay band or a benchmark. See Compensation in the Add Role window below.
  6. Review. Read the whole job description, then select Save as Draft or Activate.

If something is missing or invalid, a summary at the top of the window lists what to fix. Select an item to jump to its field. To activate a role you need at least a title, a summary, and one essential duty. You can always save a draft first and finish later. If a save is refused, for example because the title is already used, the app shows the reason and keeps your answers.

Employer classification is not a legal determination

The overtime and FLSA fields in Employment guidance are your company’s own classification. EPN does not decide whether a position is exempt or non-exempt, or eligible for overtime. Review each classification with your payroll or legal advisor.

Open a role

Section titled “Open a role”

Select a role’s title, or choose Actions → View, to open it. The role has these tabs:

Tab What it shows
Overview The role’s details, summary, and employment guidance.
Responsibilities Purpose, duties, outcomes, and relationships.
Qualifications Education, experience, skills, and working conditions.
Assigned Employees The employees who currently have the role, by name and employee number. Pay is never shown here.
Version History Every version of the role. Select View to read a past version.
Audit History Who changed the role and when, newest first. It lists which fields changed, never their values.
Compensation The role’s employer pay band and its market benchmark, kept apart. It never shows any employee’s pay. See below.

Compensation: pay band and market benchmark

Section titled “Compensation: pay band and market benchmark”

Informational only

A pay band or a market benchmark is not a promised salary, not legal advice, and not a decision about minimum wage, overtime, or any other wage law. Neither one sets anyone’s pay, and neither one replaces any minimum wage that applies.

The Compensation tab keeps two different things apart, and a third is never shown there:

What What it is
Employer pay band Your company’s own pay range for the role. You decide it.
Market benchmark Reference figures that a data source reports for the role’s occupation, shown together with that source.
An employee’s actual pay Lives in the employee’s pay rate. It is never shown on a role page, and a pay band or benchmark never changes it.

Employer pay band

Section titled “Employer pay band”

The Employer pay band card shows the band as a range, with its minimum, midpoint, maximum, the annual hours it assumes, the date it has applied since, and an optional note. It also shows the equivalent hourly or annual range at those hours, so you can see how the conversion works. A role with no band says Not set. Anyone who can view roles can see the band.

Setting or changing a band needs its own permission, separate from viewing roles. The buttons can be visible even if you don’t have it. If a change isn’t allowed, the card tells you that you can view the pay band but not change it.

  1. Select Set pay band (or Edit pay band when there is one).
  2. Choose Hourly or Annual, then enter the Minimum, Midpoint, and Maximum. Each must be above zero, with up to two decimals, and the minimum can’t be higher than the midpoint, nor the midpoint higher than the maximum.
  3. Check Annual hours assumption. It is used only to convert the band between hourly and annual amounts. 2,080 is a 40-hour week for 52 weeks. You can change it.
  4. Add a Note if you want (up to 500 characters, with no personal information about any person).
  5. Check the box that says you have reviewed the pay band against applicable minimum-wage, overtime, pay-transparency, and equal-pay requirements. You have to check it every time you save a band; the band can’t be saved without it.
  6. Select Save pay band. If something is missing or out of order, a summary at the top of the form lists it and each item takes you to its field.

Saving a change doesn’t overwrite the old band. The current band is closed and a new one is added, and Show pay band history lists every band the role has had, newest first. If someone else changed the band while you were editing, the app tells you your changes were not saved and shows the latest band. End pay band asks you to confirm, then leaves the role with no current band; the band stays in the history. An archived role keeps its band, but a new band can’t be set until the role is restored.

Market benchmark

Section titled “Market benchmark”

The Market benchmark card looks up reference figures by the role’s SOC code, the occupation code you can enter in the role’s details. It is a reference, not a recommendation: it never fills in a pay band and never suggests a pay rate.

  • Use Geography (national, a state, or a metro area), State, Reference year, Show values as (hourly or annual), and Annual hours, then select Update benchmarks.
  • Each source gets its own table, and sources are never combined into one figure. A table shows Market Low, Market Median, and Market High with the percentile the source really reports (for example the 10th, 50th, and 90th, or the 25th, 50th, and 75th), the value as the source reports it, and the value shown in the hourly or annual view you chose. It also names the source, edition, reference year, when it was retrieved and last updated, whether all three points are reported, and how to credit the source.
  • If the source has no figure for the place you asked for, the app uses the next wider area and says so (a metro area falls back to its state, then to the whole country). It never invents a local figure.
  • Hourly and annual values are converted only by multiplying or dividing by the annual hours shown. Nothing else is adjusted.
  • EPN suggested range always says Unavailable, with the reason: no method for combining sources has been approved.
  • A source’s limits are listed with its figures. Percentiles are survey estimates, not the lowest or highest pay that exists.

Benchmarks may be unavailable

Market figures appear only after an approved dataset has been imported into EPN, and EPN has not imported one yet. Until then the card says so and shows no number. A role with no SOC code says No SOC code on this role, and a role whose occupation isn’t in any imported dataset says No benchmark data to show. In both cases nothing is estimated in its place. Show benchmark sources lists the sources EPN knows about and whether each is enabled.

Viewing market benchmarks also needs its own permission. Without it the card says you don’t have permission and shows nothing, and the pay band card keeps working. Benchmark figures shown while EPN is being tested are labeled SYNTHETIC TEST DATA; they are generated for testing and are not market data.

Compensation in the Add Role window

Section titled “Compensation in the Add Role window”

The optional Compensation step shows the same pay band form and the same benchmark view. A role has to exist before it can be looked up, so:

  • A pay band you enter is kept in the window and saved with the role when you choose Save as Draft or Activate. If it can’t be saved (for example you don’t have permission to set pay bands), the role is still saved and the message tells you the band was not set, so you can set it later from the role’s Compensation tab.
  • To see benchmarks for a new role, select Save draft and look up benchmarks. The window stays open, the role becomes a draft, and your later saves update that same role.

Change a role

Section titled “Change a role”

Only a draft can be edited. To change a role that is already active:

  1. Open the role and select Create New Version. This opens a draft copy of the current version.
  2. Select Edit draft, make your changes, and select Save as Draft.
  3. Select Activate and confirm. The draft becomes the current version.

The previous version stays in the version history, and each assignment keeps pointing at the version that applied when it was made. A role can have only one open draft at a time. If someone else saved the draft while you were editing, the app tells you your changes were not saved and offers Reload latest version.

Actions on each row

Section titled “Actions on each row”

Choose Actions on a row. What you see depends on the role’s status:

Status Actions
Draft View, Edit, Duplicate, Archive
Active View, Duplicate, Create New Version (or Edit when a draft is open), Assign Employees, View Assigned Employees, Archive
Archived View, Duplicate, View Assigned Employees, Restore

Duplicate makes a new draft copy under a new title. The original isn’t changed.

Archive and restore

Section titled “Archive and restore”

Roles are never deleted. Archive asks you to confirm and explains what happens: employees who already have the role keep it, it shows as archived on their profile, and it no longer appears when you add an employee. Select Restore on an archived role that has been published to make it active again.

Give employees a role

Section titled “Give employees a role”
  • When you add an employee, choose their Role in the Add Employee window. See Add an employee.
  • For existing employees, choose Actions → Assign Employees on an active role, pick the employees, and enter an optional effective date and reason. Only an active role can be given to an employee.
  • An employee’s profile shows their current Job role under Work Details, with View role assignment history. A change of role never overwrites the past: the earlier role is kept in the history with its dates.

All Employees shows each employee’s role and can filter and search by it. See View and manage employees.

Who can do this

Section titled “Who can do this”

Viewing roles needs permission to view roles. Adding, editing, archiving, and assigning each need their own permission. CompanyAdmin and HRPayrollAdmin have all of them, and Accountants can view only. Buttons can be visible even when your role doesn’t allow the action. If a change isn’t allowed, the page tells you that you can view roles but not change them, and hides the change buttons for the rest of your visit. Ask an administrator for access.

An employer pay band is visible to anyone who can view roles. Setting, changing, or ending a pay band, and viewing market benchmarks, each need a separate permission that is not yet assigned to the standard access roles. Until it is, expect to see the band but not to change it, and a dash in Benchmark Status.
